Further source comparison found [`SBDSQR`](https://github.com/Reference-LAPACK/lapack/blob/v3.12.0/SRC/sbdsqr.f) calls [`SLARTG`](https://github.com/Reference-LAPACK/lapack/blob/v3.12.0/SRC/slartg.f90), whereas the port implemented SLARTGP's nonnegative-r convention. SLARTG keeps its cosine nonnegative and signs r like f. The combined candidate followed SLARTG, including its scaled branch. The signed (-3,4) rotation control distinguishes these conventions.

[`SGESDD` path 5s](https://github.com/Reference-LAPACK/lapack/blob/v3.12.0/SRC/sgesdd.f) applies Q to the bidiagonal left vectors through SORMBR, which uses [SORM2R](https://github.com/Reference-LAPACK/lapack/blob/v3.12.0/SRC/sorm2r.f) at this size. The port previously materialized Q with ORG2R and multiplied Q*Ub. That candidate applied the reflectors directly; this hypothesis is now tested individually.

The SciPy oracle is linked to OpenBLAS 0.3.31.dev (reported by `scipy.show_config()` on i265). Its BLAS accumulation is separate from the LAPACK control flow. Inspection of the [OpenBLAS x86 SNRM2 kernel](https://github.com/OpenMathLib/OpenBLAS/blob/v0.3.30/kernel/x86_64/nrm2_sse.S) shows f32 inputs converted to f64 before squaring, accumulation and square root. The former Rust scaled-f32 norm rounded each division/addition; the rejected experimental norm accumulated squares in f64 and rounded the result once to f32. This is an oracle-specific BLAS convention, not a claim that every LAPACK build draws the same basis. Kernel grouping has not been claimed bit-identical.
